# Product commercialization, research and next steps after the hackathon

## Strategic objective

Transition from award-focused MVP to a learning tool students choose to use repeatedly. The priority is **retained learning benefit**, not maximal engagement or fast feature inflation.

## First 30 days after submission

- Invite a small closed-alpha cohort spanning different study preferences and access needs.
- Observe how they create subjects, navigate paths, work through lessons and return.
- Fix import quality, source correctness, assessment trust and onboarding confusion before adding dozens of widgets.
- Produce measured cost profile: model usage per new source, lesson, tutor turn and weekly active student.
- Run baseline usability and mastery-evidence evaluations; document known shortcomings honestly.

## Next 60–90 days

- Finish Phase 2 durable library/editing/responsive features, then Phase 3 knowledge graph/retention.
- Deploy modest XP and trophy experiments with guardrail metrics (real learning and study stress).
- Evolve collaboration based on whether students actually use existing shared rooms.
- Investigate educator partnerships to verify grading and source fidelity.

## Monetization hypotheses to test

Free subject/file limits with source ownership and accessibility always intact; Plus adds higher AI/source limits and richer personalization; Groups offers collaborative management; institutional version offers classroom/organization tools. Conduct willingness-to-pay testing before setting prices. Avoid selling fabricated credentials or locking export rights behind a plan.

## Adoption and growth loops

Share meaningful study artifacts and invites (not personal mistakes or score rankings), collectible subject worlds and progress celebrations, groups invited to study on a shared board. Referral campaigns later; privacy-first defaults.

## Durable differentiation

The moat is **validated conversion of student material into a spatial learning process**, combined with meaningful adaptive practice and social knowledge objects—not the inherited OurSpaces visual style alone. Deep learning outcomes and proprietary user-earned artifacts may sustain retention, but require empirical verification.
